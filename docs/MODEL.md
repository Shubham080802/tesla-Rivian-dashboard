# Method notes

## Data
* **Tesla volumes:** deliveries and production by quarter from Tesla's 8-K Exhibit 99.1 releases (datahub.io snapshot, Q2 2019 onward, plus the latest quarter read from the filing). **Tesla financials:** SEC EDGAR company facts; missing quarters are filled only by accounting identities (Q2 = six months minus Q1, and so on), never estimated.
* **Attention:** English Wikipedia monthly page views (Tesla = "Tesla, Inc." plus "Tesla Motors"; Rivian = "Rivian"), complete months only.
* **Charging:** public Tesla-network stations open today, by opening year, from the U.S. federal station locator, so closed stations are missing and older years are understated.

* **Rivian:** production and deliveries by quarter from its 8-K releases (Q1 2022 onward; every year's quarters sum exactly to the reported annual totals, and Q3 2026 was read from the 2 October 2026 8-K). Financials from SEC EDGAR, filled only by accounting identities, like Tesla. Its charging stations are the public Rivian Adventure Network sites in the U.S. federal station locator; attention is the English Wikipedia "Rivian" article; share price changes use RIVN (derived percentages only). Rivian's forecasts rest on 19 quarters, so their ranges are wide and labelled that way; revenue growth is not shown for 2022 because 2021 revenue was only $55 million.

## Forecasts (`forecast_engine.py`)
Four exponential-smoothing candidates (level, damped trend, seasonality, trend plus seasonality) are chosen by rolling-origin backtest. Volumes are modelled on a log scale; 1,000 simulated paths use seeded bootstrap shocks; the 80% range is calibrated to its backtest coverage (scale 0.6 to 3.0). The engine also stores one-step-ahead forecasts for the last periods (the "on track" chart), a seasonal index, and 19 cumulative quantiles used for "chance of growth" and "chance of reaching a target". Both companies are modelled quarterly (season 4). Rivian's history is short, so its ranges are wide and labelled that way.

## Outlook rule
"Chance of growth" is the probability that the next 12 months exceed the last 12, read from the stored quantiles. **Expanding** needs 70% or more, **Contracting** 30% or less, otherwise **Uncertain**. A series whose backtest error is above 30% is marked tentative.

## Where they sell (`scripts/build_regions.py`)
Rivian sells almost only in North America and reports no regional split, so this tab is Tesla only. Tesla China: CPCA monthly figures as tabulated by CnEVPost, January 2024 to September 2026. Domestic is deliveries in China and exports are Shanghai plant shipments abroad; their sum is CPCA's wholesale. The typed-in figures reconcile exactly to the reported July 2026 wholesale (93,579), Q3 2026 (275,111), September 2025 (90,812) and the nine-month 2026 total (743,060); August 2026 domestic is derived from the reported wholesale minus exports. Europe (EU, EFTA and UK): Tesla's row in the "new car registrations by manufacturer" table in ACEA's own monthly press releases (`acea.auto/files/Press_release_car_registrations_<Month>_2026.pdf`), January to August 2026, read into `data/external/ev/acea_primary_rows.csv`. The 2025 months are restated from the prior-year columns of those releases (ACEA revises earlier months); 2024 and the 2025 months not restated come from CnEVPost's table of ACEA data, which matches the releases exactly for January and February 2026 and to within 0.9% for 2025. July 2026 has no release of its own and is derived from the January to August year-to-date total minus January to June minus August. Registrations are not sales or deliveries. 2026 months are as first published, so ACEA's later revisions are not applied: Tesla's January to June sum is 0.1% from ACEA's latest year-to-date total. `scripts/check_data.py` audits the series against the primary rows.

## Stock vs business (`scripts/build_stocks.py`)
No free monthly price source clearly allows republishing prices (Alpha Vantage and Yahoo restrict redistribution, Nasdaq data is licensed, and Stooq now needs a key from a CAPTCHA). The script fetches monthly split-adjusted closes (TSLA and RIVN, both in US dollars) from Yahoo Finance's chart endpoint, which is personal-use data and an unofficial API, so it is run by hand, keeps the raw closes in a git-ignored local cache, and commits and publishes only derived figures: calendar-year price changes (year-end close to year-end close), the year-to-date change to the last full month, and annualised volatility of the last 36 monthly moves. Dividends are excluded. Tesla's yearly changes are checked against the published record (2020 +743%, 2021 +50%, 2022 -65%, 2023 +102%, 2024 +63%). The tab sets them beside revenue growth (SEC filings) and volume growth. Five or six yearly points cannot show that prices follow the business, so the tab only shows where the market ran ahead of or behind revenue. It is not part of the daily GitHub Action because Yahoo's terms forbid automated collection and its endpoint often blocks cloud runners. Not investment advice.

## Demand drivers (`scripts/build_drivers.py`)
U.S. indicators from FRED tested against year-over-year growth in Tesla deliveries (quarterly, 26 points) and Rivian deliveries (quarterly, 15 points), at the same period and with the indicator leading by one period. Overlapping year-over-year windows overstate significance, so the p-value that counts uses an effective sample of n/4 and a Bonferroni correction. "Yes" needs that to pass; a raw p below 0.05 alone is "Suggestive". Nothing passes today. The indicators are context, not inputs to the forecast.

## Known limits
No customer reviews or ratings (no open data), no monthly volumes (both companies report quarterly), no raw stock prices, and no U.S. registrations. Companies listed outside the U.S. (BYD, Chinese and European makers) are deliberately out of scope. Volumes are not revenue. These are public figures and not investment advice.
